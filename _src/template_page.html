3 Wire RTD vs 4 Wire RTD

Side by side comparison: how they work, where each fits, and which to choose.

Quick answer: 3 wire RTDs cancel lead resistance if all leads are equal and are standard in plants; 4 wire RTDs remove lead effects completely for precision work.

Choose 3 Wire RTD when

  • Normal process RTDs with head or field transmitters

Choose 4 Wire RTD when

  • Reference thermometers, high accuracy, long or unequal leads

Key Differences Explained

In a 2 wire circuit the lead resistance adds directly: 0.34 Ω of copper adds about 0.9 °C on a Pt100. A 3 wire bridge subtracts one lead from the other. A 4 wire circuit drives current on two wires and measures voltage on the other two, where almost no current flows.

Head mount transmitters keep leads short, so 3 wire is usually enough.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between 3 Wire RTD and 4 Wire RTD?

3 wire RTDs cancel lead resistance if all leads are equal and are standard in plants; 4 wire RTDs remove lead effects completely for precision work.

Can I use a 4 wire sensor on a 3 wire input?

Yes, leave one lead unconnected.

Why do 3 wire leads need to be equal?

The method assumes the two current carrying leads have the same resistance.

Is 2 wire ever acceptable?

With Pt1000 or very short leads, or where errors of a degree do not matter.
