How a Displacer Level Transmitter Works

Raise the level and change the density. Watch the buoyancy force grow and the apparent weight of the displacer fall.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: A displacer level transmitter hangs a heavy cylinder (the displacer) in the liquid. As level rises the liquid pushes it up with a buoyancy force, its apparent weight falls, and a torque tube or spring converts that change into a level signal.

Displacer Level Transmitter Working Principle Step by Step

  1. The displacer hangs on a torque arm inside a cage connected to the vessel.
  2. It is heavier than the liquid, so it hardly moves; it does not float like a float switch.
  3. Liquid around it pushes up with a force equal to the weight of liquid displaced (Archimedes).
  4. The torque tube twists less as buoyancy grows, and the transmitter measures this tiny rotation.
  5. Output is calibrated for a given density, so a density change shifts the reading.

Displacer Level Transmitter Formula

F(b) = ρ × g × A × h(sub)
Apparent weight = W − F(b)
A = π D² ÷ 4 (displacer area)
Example: 76 mm displacer fully covered in water: F(b) ≈ 15.9 N

Main Parts of a Displacer Level Transmitter

PartWhat it does
DisplacerSealed cylinder sized for the level range and density
Torque tubeHollow tube that twists with load and seals the process
CageExternal chamber with side connections to the vessel
TransmitterConverts rotation to 4 to 20 mA level

Advantages

  • Proven for level and interface in high pressure and temperature
  • Works with foam and turbulent surfaces
  • Can measure interface between two liquids

Limitations

  • Reading depends on density
  • Moving and wearing parts, heavy to handle
  • Range limited to the displacer length

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the displacer float?

No. It is heavier than the liquid at every level. It only gets lighter as more of it is covered.

Can a displacer measure interface?

Yes. When fully submerged, the buoyancy depends on how much of each liquid surrounds it, which gives the interface level.

Why does density matter?

Buoyancy is proportional to density, so the transmitter must be calibrated for the actual liquid or corrected.

How is it calibrated?

Often with weights that simulate the buoyancy at each level, or by filling the cage with the process liquid or water and correcting for density.
