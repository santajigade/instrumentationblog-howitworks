How a Bimetal Temperature Switch Works

Move the set point and the differential and watch the water tank cycle between the two. Change the strip length to see how much the bimetal tip moves for every degree.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: A bimetal temperature switch uses two metals with different expansion bonded into one strip. As the temperature rises the strip bends, and at the set point it moves a contact that switches a heater, fan or alarm.

What Is a Bimetal Temperature Switch?

Metals grow when heated, but not all by the same amount. Brass grows roughly fifteen times more than invar for the same temperature rise, so a strip made of the two bonded together must curl toward the invar side.

A bimetal temperature switch turns that curl into a contact movement. An adjusting screw sets how far the strip must travel, and a snap mechanism makes the contact jump cleanly open and shut.

These switches need no power and switch the load directly, which is why they are found in water heaters, motors, panels and overheat cut outs. Knowing the set point, the differential and the contact rating is enough to apply most of them correctly.

Bimetal Temperature Switch Working Principle Step by Step

  1. The sensing element sits in a thermowell or against the surface, and reaches the process temperature.
  2. The brass layer expands more than the invar layer, so the bonded strip bends toward the invar side.
  3. The tip carries a moving contact that rests on a fixed contact while the strip is cool.
  4. At the set point the strip has moved far enough to lift the contact, and a snap mechanism opens it quickly to cut the heater.
  5. As the process cools the strip straightens, and the contact recloses at the set point minus the differential.

Bimetal Temperature Switch Formula

Tip deflection d = 3 × Δα × ΔT × L² ÷ (4 × t), for two layers of equal thickness and stiffness
Δα is the difference in expansion coefficients, about 17 × 10⁻⁶ per °C for brass on invar
The contact opens when d reaches the travel set by the adjusting screw
Reset point = set point − differential
Example: L 40 mm and t 0.5 mm give 3 × 0.000017 × 1600 ÷ 2 = 0.0408 mm per °C, so a 70 °C set point needs 2.04 mm of travel from 20 °C and the switch resets at 65 °C

Main Parts of a Bimetal Temperature Switch

PartWhat it does
Bimetal elementA flat strip, coil, spiral or snap disc made of two bonded metals with different expansion
ContactsMoving and fixed contacts, often silver alloy, wired as normally closed or normally open
Adjusting screw or knobMoves the fixed contact or preloads the strip to change the set point
Snap mechanismA small magnet or a domed snap disc that makes the contact jump and sets the differential
Housing and wellProtects the element and lets it be removed without draining the process

Where a Bimetal Temperature Switch Is Used

Water heaters and tanksCut the heater at the set point and switch it back on as the water cools.
Motor and transformer protectionThermal cut outs in windings open the control circuit on overheating.
Panel climateSwitch panel heaters on in cold weather and cooling fans on in hot weather.
Overheat cut outsManual reset safety cut outs on heaters, dryers and boilers.

Advantages

  • Needs no power supply and switches the load directly
  • Simple, robust and low cost
  • Snap action gives a clear on off signal with little contact chatter

Limitations

  • Lower set point accuracy and repeatability than a transmitter with a trip
  • Slow response, especially inside a thick thermowell
  • Contacts wear and have a limited current rating

Bimetal Temperature Switch Field Tips and Common Problems

  • Check the contact rating against the real load; use the switch to drive a contactor for large heaters or inductive loads.
  • Do not set the differential so small that the load cycles every few seconds; frequent switching shortens contact life.
  • Verify the set point in a calibrated bath with the switch in its own well, rising slowly, and note both the trip and the reset temperature.
  • Use heat transfer paste in the thermowell if the maker allows it, to cut the response time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the differential of a temperature switch?

It is the gap between the temperature where the contact operates on rising temperature and where it resets on falling temperature. It stops the contact from chattering when the temperature hovers near the set point.

Why does the strip bend instead of just getting longer?

Both layers are bonded along their full length, so the layer that expands more is forced to sit on the outside of a curve. The strip curls toward the layer with the lower expansion.

What is the difference between a strip and a snap disc?

A strip moves gradually with temperature and needs a magnet or toggle for clean switching. A domed snap disc flips over suddenly at its rated temperature, which gives built in snap action and a fixed differential.

Should I use a temperature switch or a transmitter?

A switch is cheaper and needs no power for a simple on off job. A transmitter gives a live value, diagnostics and an adjustable trip, which is usually preferred for control and safety functions.
