How an Ultrasonic Level Transmitter Works

Move the level and the air temperature sliders. Watch the sound pulse travel down to the liquid and back, see the echo move on the echo curve and check how much error appears without temperature compensation.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: An ultrasonic level transmitter sends a short burst of sound from a transducer at the top of the tank and listens for the echo from the liquid surface. The time of flight multiplied by the speed of sound and divided by two gives the distance, and level is the reference height minus that distance.

What Is an Ultrasonic Level Transmitter?

Ultrasonic level measurement is non contact: only the sound pulse touches the liquid. That makes it a simple choice for water, wastewater, slurries and many chemicals in open or vented tanks.

The transducer is a piezoelectric element that works as both loudspeaker and microphone. It sends a burst at an ultrasonic frequency, then switches to listening and waits for the surface echo.

Sound travels through the air above the liquid, and its speed changes with air temperature. For that reason almost every ultrasonic transmitter has a built in temperature sensor and corrects the speed of sound on every reading.

Ultrasonic Level Transmitter Working Principle Step by Step

  1. The electronics drive the piezo transducer with a short burst of ultrasonic sound aimed straight down at the liquid.
  2. The transducer rings for a short time after the burst, so echoes from inside the blanking distance cannot be read.
  3. The pulse spreads in a narrow cone, reflects at the liquid surface and returns to the transducer, which now acts as a receiver.
  4. The transmitter finds the surface echo on its echo curve, measures the time of flight and corrects the speed of sound with the measured air temperature.
  5. Distance is converted to level, and level is sent as 4 to 20 mA, HART or a fieldbus value, often with volume or open channel flow calculated in the transmitter.

Ultrasonic Level Transmitter Formula

Distance d = c × t ÷ 2, where t is the round trip time of flight
Speed of sound in air c = 331.3 × √(1 + T ÷ 273.15) m/s, with T in °C
Level = H − d, where H is the distance from the transducer face to 0 %
Output = 4 + 16 × level ÷ span mA
Example: H 6 m, level 2.50 m, 20 °C: c = 343.2 m/s, d = 3.50 m, t = 7.00 ÷ 343.2 = 20.40 ms, output 12.00 mA; if the air is at 40 °C but the transmitter assumes 20 °C, it reads 2.614 m, an error of 0.114 m

Main Parts of an Ultrasonic Level Transmitter

Blanking distance settingIgnores the zone just below the face where the transducer is still ringing
Output stageGives 4 to 20 mA, relays or digital communication to the PLC or DCS

Where an Ultrasonic Level Transmitter Is Used

Water and wastewaterWet wells, sumps, clarifiers and reservoirs where non contact sensing avoids fouling.
Open channel flowLevel over a flume or weir is converted to flow inside the transmitter.
Chemical storageVented tanks of acids, alkalis and other liquids that would attack a probe.
Bulk solidsBins and hoppers with coarse material, using a low frequency transducer.

Advantages

  • Non contact, so no wetted parts and nothing to coat or corrode
  • Simple to install on top of open or vented tanks
  • Built in calculations for volume and open channel flow

Limitations

  • Foam, heavy vapour, dust or a turbulent surface can absorb or scatter the echo
  • Cannot work in vacuum and is affected by gases other than air
  • Blanking distance means the highest level must stay below the face by a set margin

Ultrasonic Level Transmitter Field Tips and Common Problems

  • Mount the transducer away from the tank wall, inlet streams and ladders so the beam cone sees only the liquid surface.
  • Keep the maximum level below the blanking distance, or use a standpipe or extension nozzle to raise the transducer.
  • Shade the transducer from direct sun, because heating at the face makes the internal temperature reading differ from the air in the tank.
  • Run a false echo map with the tank empty or low, so fixed objects inside the beam are suppressed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does an ultrasonic level transmitter need temperature compensation?

The speed of sound in air rises by about 0.6 m/s for each °C. Without correction a 20 °C change gives an error of a few percent of the measured distance.

What is the blanking distance?

After sending the burst the transducer keeps vibrating for a short time and cannot hear echoes. Any surface closer than this distance is not measured, so the 100 % level must be set below it.

Can an ultrasonic transmitter measure through foam?

Thick or soft foam absorbs sound and may give no echo or an echo from the foam top. Radar or guided wave radar is usually a better choice for foaming liquids.

What is the difference between ultrasonic and radar level?

Ultrasonic uses sound waves that need air or gas to travel and depend on temperature. Radar uses microwaves that are not affected by vapour temperature or pressure in the same way and also work in vacuum.
