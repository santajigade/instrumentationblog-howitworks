How an Orifice Plate Works

Move the sliders to change flow and beta ratio. Watch the liquid speed up through the bore, the pressure dip at the vena contracta and the DP transmitter respond.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: An orifice plate is a thin plate with a hole that squeezes the flow. The liquid speeds up through the bore, its pressure falls, and the differential pressure (DP) across the plate rises with the square of the flow rate.

Orifice Plate Working Principle Step by Step

  1. The plate is clamped between two flanges and the bore is smaller than the pipe.
  2. Liquid accelerates to pass through the bore, so velocity rises and static pressure falls (Bernoulli).
  3. Just after the plate the jet is narrowest (vena contracta) and pressure is lowest.
  4. Two taps feed the DP transmitter: DP grows with the square of flow, so 50 % flow gives only 25 % DP.
  5. The transmitter or the control system takes the square root of DP to get a linear flow signal.

Orifice Plate Formula

Q = C × E × (π d² ÷ 4) × √(2 ΔP ÷ ρ)
E = 1 ÷ √(1 − β⁴) and β = d ÷ D
So Q ∝ √ΔP and ΔP ∝ Q²
Example: 70 % flow gives 0.7² = 49 % of full scale DP

Main Parts of an Orifice Plate

PartWhat it does
Orifice plateThin plate with a precise sharp edged bore, usually stainless steel
Flanges and tapsHold the plate and provide the H and L pressure connections
Impulse linesSmall tubes that carry the two pressures to the transmitter
DP transmitterMeasures ΔP and sends a 4 to 20 mA flow signal
Straight pipe runUpstream and downstream lengths that give a settled flow profile

Advantages

  • Simple, cheap and works on liquids, gases and steam
  • Standardised in ISO 5167, so it can be calculated without flow calibration
  • No moving parts in the line

Limitations

  • Permanent pressure loss costs pumping energy
  • Square root relation limits rangeability to about 3:1 or 4:1
  • Needs long straight runs and a sharp, clean edge

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does DP rise with the square of flow?

Pressure drop comes from the kinetic energy of the fluid, which depends on velocity squared. Double the flow and the DP rises four times.

What is the beta ratio?

It is the bore diameter divided by the pipe inside diameter. Most plates use a beta ratio between about 0.2 and 0.75.

What is the vena contracta?

It is the narrowest point of the jet just downstream of the plate, where velocity is highest and pressure is lowest.

Is all the pressure drop lost?

No. Part of it recovers downstream. A rough estimate of the permanent loss is (1 − β^1.9) times the measured DP.
