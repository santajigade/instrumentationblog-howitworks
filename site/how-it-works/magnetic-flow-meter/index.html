How a Magnetic Flow Meter Works

Change the velocity and pipe size and watch the charges in the liquid push towards the electrodes. The voltage they create is what the meter measures.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: A magnetic flow meter (magmeter) uses Faraday's law. Coils create a magnetic field across the pipe, and a conductive liquid moving through it produces a small voltage at two electrodes that is proportional to the average velocity.

What Is a Magnetic Flow Meter?

Magnetic flow meters are the standard choice for water, wastewater, slurries and most water based chemicals. They have no obstruction in the pipe, so pressure loss is almost zero and solids pass straight through.

The meter measures velocity, not mass, and it only works on liquids that conduct electricity. Accuracy of about 0.2 % to 0.5 % of reading is typical for a well installed meter.

Most problems with magmeters come from installation rather than the sensor: poor grounding, a partly empty pipe or electrodes coated with deposits.

Magnetic Flow Meter Working Principle Step by Step

  1. Two coils on the outside of the pipe create a magnetic field across the flow.
  2. The pipe is lined with an insulating liner so the voltage is not shorted by the metal pipe.
  3. As conductive liquid moves through the field, positive and negative charges are pushed to opposite sides.
  4. Two electrodes pick up this voltage; it rises in direct proportion to the average velocity.
  5. The field is reversed many times per second (pulsed DC) so the meter can cancel electrode offsets and noise.

Magnetic Flow Meter Formula

E = B × D × v
E: electrode voltage (V) B: field (T)
D: inside diameter (m) v: mean velocity (m/s)
Q = v × π D² ÷ 4
Example: 2 mT × 0.1 m × 2 m/s = 0.4 mV

Main Parts of a Magnetic Flow Meter

PartWhat it does
Field coilsCreate the magnetic field, driven by the transmitter with pulsed DC
LinerPTFE, PFA, rubber or ceramic layer that insulates the pipe
ElectrodesMetal pins in contact with the liquid that pick up the voltage
Grounding rings or electrodeTie the liquid to the meter reference to stop noise
TransmitterDrives the coils, measures microvolts and outputs flow

Where a Magnetic Flow Meter Is Used

Water and wastewaterRaw water, treated water, sludge and effluent lines.
Slurries and pulpMining slurries and paper stock, using abrasion resistant liners.
Chemical dosingAcids, alkalis and other conductive chemicals with PTFE or PFA liners.
Food and beverageMilk, juice and beer with hygienic connections.

Advantages

  • No obstruction and no pressure loss
  • Accuracy is not affected by density, viscosity or temperature
  • Ideal for slurries, water and wastewater

Limitations

  • Liquid must be conductive (typically above about 5 µS/cm)
  • Cannot measure gases, steam or hydrocarbons
  • Needs a full pipe and good grounding

Magnetic Flow Meter Field Tips and Common Problems

  • Mount the meter where the pipe is always full, such as a vertical pipe with upward flow.
  • Use grounding rings or a grounding electrode on plastic or lined pipes.
  • Keep the electrodes in the horizontal plane so air bubbles at the top do not touch them.
  • If the reading is noisy, check conductivity, grounding and nearby VFD cables first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why must the liquid be conductive?

The voltage is generated by charges in the liquid. Without enough conductivity there is no measurable signal, which is why oils and pure hydrocarbons cannot be measured.

Why is the field reversed?

Reversing the field lets the transmitter separate the flow signal from steady electrode offsets and slow drift, giving a stable zero.

Does the meter measure mass flow?

No. It measures velocity and therefore volume flow. Mass flow needs a separate density value.

Why does an empty pipe cause errors?

With a partly full pipe the electrodes can lose contact with the liquid, so the reading becomes noisy or wrong. Many meters have empty pipe detection.
