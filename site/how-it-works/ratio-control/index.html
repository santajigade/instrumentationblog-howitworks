How Ratio Control Works

Set the required ratio, the average wild flow and the span of the controlled flow transmitter. Watch the ratio station follow the wild flow and the second flow loop track its moving setpoint.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: Ratio control keeps one flow in a fixed proportion to another. The uncontrolled or wild flow is measured, multiplied by the ratio R, and the result becomes the remote setpoint of a flow controller on the second stream.

What Is a Ratio Control?

Many processes need two streams in a fixed proportion: fuel and air in a burner, acid and water in dilution, or two reactants in a blend. The main stream is often set by an upstream unit, so the plant can only measure it.

Ratio control measures this wild flow and calculates the setpoint for the controlled flow as R times the wild flow. When the wild flow rises, the controlled flow follows at once, before any quality analyser sees a change.

In a DCS this is built with a ratio station or a multiplier block feeding a flow controller in cascade mode. The operator usually enters the ratio, and an outer quality loop can trim it.

Ratio Control Working Principle Step by Step

  1. The flow transmitter on stream A measures the wild flow, which is set by the upstream process.
  2. The ratio station multiplies this measurement by the ratio R entered by the operator or by an outer loop.
  3. The result is sent as a remote setpoint to the flow controller on stream B, which runs in cascade mode.
  4. The flow controller compares flow B with its setpoint and moves the control valve to remove the error.
  5. When flow A rises or falls, the setpoint for B moves with it, so the blend keeps the same proportion.

Ratio Control Formula

Setpoint of flow B = R × flow A
Blend flow = flow A × (1 + R); share of B in the blend = R ÷ (1 + R)
When both signals are in percent of span: K = R × span A ÷ span B
For square root DP meters, linearise both signals before the multiplier
Example: R 0.5, flow A 60 m³/h, span A 100 m³/h, span B 50 m³/h: setpoint B = 0.5 × 60 = 30 m³/h, blend = 90 m³/h, share of B = 0.5 ÷ 1.5 = 33.3 %, K = 0.5 × 100 ÷ 50 = 1.0

Main Parts of a Ratio Control

PartWhat it does
Wild flow transmitterMeasures the stream that the loop cannot or should not control
Ratio station or multiplierMultiplies the wild flow by R, often with a bias, and passes it on as a setpoint
Flow controllerPI controller on the second stream, in cascade mode so it accepts the remote setpoint
Flow transmitter on stream BMeasures the controlled flow for the flow controller
Control valveFinal element on the controlled stream

Where a Ratio Control Is Used

Combustion air and fuelAir flow follows fuel flow to keep the right excess air.
Chemical dosingDosing pump or valve follows the main water flow.
BlendingComponents of a fuel or product blend stay in fixed proportions.
Reflux and distillateReflux flow is held in ratio to the distillate or feed flow.

Advantages

  • Corrects for changes in the wild flow before the product quality moves
  • Simple to build with standard DCS blocks
  • Operator works with a meaningful number, the ratio, instead of a flow setpoint

Limitations

  • Accuracy depends on both flow measurements
  • A failed wild flow transmitter drives the controlled flow to a wrong value
  • Needs an outer trim loop when the true required ratio changes with the feed

Ratio Control Field Tips and Common Problems

  • Check that both flow signals are linear in engineering units before the multiplier; mixing a square root signal with a linear one gives a wrong ratio.
  • Scale the ratio when the DCS works in percent of span, using K = R × span A ÷ span B.
  • Tune the flow controller on stream B in auto first, then switch it to cascade.
  • Add a low and high clamp on the calculated setpoint so a transmitter fault cannot open the valve fully.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a wild flow?

It is the flow that the ratio loop measures but does not control. It is set by another unit, a level controller or the operator.

Is ratio control the same as cascade control?

It uses the same remote setpoint idea, but the setpoint comes from a measured flow times R instead of from a master controller output.

Why divide the spans when the DCS works in percent?

A percent of span value from each transmitter has a different meaning. Multiplying R by span A ÷ span B converts the ratio so the percentages give the right flow.

How is ratio control used in burners?

Air flow is set in ratio to fuel flow, often with cross limits so air leads on load increase and fuel leads on load decrease.
