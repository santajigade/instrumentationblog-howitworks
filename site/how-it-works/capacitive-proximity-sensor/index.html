How a Capacitive Proximity Sensor Works

Set the rated sensing distance, the material correction factor of the target and the gap to the passing boxes. Watch the oscillator build up as a target enters the field and see when the output switches reliably.

Live animationAdjustable slidersFormula and example

Quick answer: A capacitive proximity sensor builds an electric field in front of its face between an active electrode and an earth electrode. Any target with a dielectric constant higher than air raises the capacitance, the oscillator starts to swing above a threshold and the output switches.

What Is a Capacitive Proximity Sensor?

A capacitive proximity sensor detects almost any material without touching it: plastic, glass, wood, paper, grain, powder, water and metal. That makes it the usual choice when the target is not metal, or when a level must be seen through the wall of a plastic tank or chute.

Inside the sensing face sit two electrodes that form an open capacitor, connected to an RC oscillator. A target in front of the face adds capacitance, the oscillator starts to swing, and a trigger turns that change into a clean on off output.

How far the sensor reaches depends on the dielectric constant of the target, so the rated distance on the datasheet applies only to an earthed metal plate or water. Most installations also need a sensitivity adjustment on site, done with the real target in place.

Capacitive Proximity Sensor Working Principle Step by Step

  1. The active electrode and the earth electrode at the face form an open capacitor, and their electric field spreads out in front of the sensor.
  2. With only air in the field the capacitance is small, so the RC oscillator stays below its oscillation point or swings with a very small amplitude.
  3. A target entering the field has a dielectric constant higher than air; its molecules polarise and the capacitance between the electrodes rises.
  4. The extra capacitance lets the oscillator build up; when its amplitude crosses the trigger threshold the output transistor switches.
  5. When the target leaves, the amplitude falls again; a small hysteresis keeps the output from chattering, and the potentiometer sets how much capacitance change is needed.

Capacitive Proximity Sensor Formula

C = ε0 × εr × A ÷ d (a higher εr or a smaller gap gives a higher capacitance)
Real switching distance Sr = Sn × material correction factor
Assured operating distance Sa = 0.81 × Sn × factor (allows ±10 % unit spread and ±10 % drift)
Typical factors: metal and water about 1.0, glass about 0.5, PVC and wood about 0.2 to 0.6, oil about 0.1 (use the datasheet curve)
Example: Sn 15 mm, factor 0.5: Sr = 7.5 mm and Sa = 0.81 × 7.5 = 6.1 mm, so a box at a 5 mm gap is detected reliably

Main Parts of a Capacitive Proximity Sensor

PartWhat it does
Active and earth electrodesForm an open capacitor whose field reaches out in front of the sensing face
RC oscillatorStarts to swing as the capacitance rises; its amplitude is the quantity the target changes
Sensitivity potentiometerSets the switching point so the sensor sees the target but ignores the wall or dust
Trigger and output stageCompares the amplitude with a threshold, adds hysteresis and drives a PNP or NPN transistor
Housing and LEDPlastic or metal barrel for mounting, with an LED that shows the switching state

Where a Capacitive Proximity Sensor Is Used

Level through a tank wallDetects liquid or granules behind a plastic or glass wall without any process connection.
Packaging linesSenses cartons, bottles, paper stacks and plastic parts that an inductive sensor cannot see.
Bulk solidsGives high and low level alarms for grain, pellets and powder in hoppers and silos.
Presence of liquid in tubesChecks for liquid in a transparent hose or a pipette on filling and dosing machines.

Advantages

  • Detects metal and non metal targets, liquids and solids
  • Can sense through a thin non metal wall
  • Non contact, with no mechanical wear

Limitations

  • Range depends strongly on the target material and must be adjusted on site
  • Humidity, condensation and build up on the face can cause false switching
  • Shorter range and slower switching than an inductive sensor of the same size

Capacitive Proximity Sensor Field Tips and Common Problems

  • Adjust the potentiometer with the real target in place, then back it off until the empty state switches off cleanly with some margin.
  • For level through a tank wall, keep the wall thickness within what the datasheet allows and press the face firmly against it.
  • Keep the face clean; a film of wet product or dust can look like a target and hold the output on.
  • Follow the flush and non flush mounting rules, and keep the spacing between two sensors so their fields do not interact.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between inductive and capacitive proximity sensors?

An inductive sensor uses a magnetic field and detects only metal. A capacitive sensor uses an electric field and detects any material whose dielectric constant is higher than air.

Why does the sensing distance change with the material?

The capacitance change depends on the dielectric constant of the target. Water and metal give a large change and the full range, while oil or dry plastic give a small change and a short range.

Can a capacitive sensor detect liquid through a pipe or tank wall?

Yes, if the wall is non metal and thin enough. The sensitivity is set so the wall alone does not switch the output but the liquid behind it does.

Why does my capacitive sensor switch on by itself?

Usually because of build up, condensation or a sensitivity set too high. Clean the face, reduce the sensitivity and check that nothing conductive sits near the field.
