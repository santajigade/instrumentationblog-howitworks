Field Check: Verify a Level Reading with Head

Head depends only on the vertical height of the liquid column and its density, not on the pipe diameter or the shape of the vessel. A narrow 10 m standpipe and a wide 10 m deep tank, both full of water, give the same 0.98 bar at the bottom. This is why a single tapping at the base of a tank can measure level, and why a small bore impulse line does not change the reading as long as it is full of the same liquid and free of trapped gas.

When you doubt a hydrostatic level transmitter, take a manual dip or tape reading, multiply the liquid height by SG and by 0.0981 to get the expected pressure in bar, and compare it with the pressure value the transmitter shows rather than its percentage output. If the pressure agrees but the level does not, the fault is in the range or SG setting. If the pressure itself is wrong, check the tapping: a blocked or gas filled impulse line, or a transmitter mounted below the tapping without a zero shift, is the usual cause.
