Pressure to Head Conversion: How to Convert Bar, PSI and kPa into Metres or Feet of Liquid Head

Pressure and head describe the same energy in two different languages, one in force per area and one in height of liquid. Learn the formula, the role of specific gravity and how to read pump gauges correctly with worked numbers.

Head is simply pressure expressed as the height of a liquid column, and the link between them is fluid density. This article shows the formula, a calculator, a conversion table and solved examples for bar, psi and kPa.

Quick Answer

How do you do pressure to head conversion for any liquid?

Pressure to head conversion uses h = P / (ρ × g), where P is pressure in pascal, ρ is liquid density in kg/m³ and g is 9.81 m/s². For water, 1 bar gives about 10.2 m of head and 1 psi gives about 2.31 ft. Example: 5 bar on water equals 500000 ÷ (1000 × 9.80665), which is roughly 51 m of head.

  1. Convert the pressure reading into pascal (1 bar = 100000 Pa, 1 psi = 6895 Pa).
  2. Find the specific gravity of the liquid at its operating temperature.
  3. Multiply specific gravity by 1000 to get density in kg/m³.
  4. Divide pressure by density times 9.81 to get head in metres.
  5. Multiply metres by 3.281 if you need head in feet.

What Is Pressure to Head Conversion?

Pressure to head conversion is the process of expressing a fluid pressure as the height of a liquid column that would create the same pressure at its base. It uses the hydrostatic relation h = P / (ρg), so the same pressure gives more head in a light liquid and less head in a heavy one.

Key Facts
  • For water at about 4 °C, 1 bar equals 10.197 m and 1 psi equals 2.307 ft of head.
  • Head in feet equals psi × 2.31 ÷ specific gravity, a rule used across the pump industry.
  • A pump develops the same head on any thin liquid, but the gauge pressure changes with specific gravity.

Before you start, it helps to revise the hydrostatic pressure formula because head is that same equation solved for height. If you are new to the subject, the article on basics of pressure measurement explains pascal, bar and psi in simple terms.

Do You Know?

The Hydraulic Institute Pump System Certification formula sheet gives the field rule h = p × 2.31 ÷ s, where h is head in feet, p is pressure in psi and s is specific gravity.

What Is the Formula for Pressure to Head?

The formula is h = P / (ρ × g), where P is pressure in pascal, ρ is density in kg/m³ and g is 9.80665 m/s². It comes directly from the hydrostatic equation P = ρgh rearranged to give height.

Hydrostatic pressure: P = ρ × g × h
Solve for head: h = P ÷ (ρ × g)
With specific gravity: h (m) = P (Pa) ÷ (SG × 9806.65)
Bar to metres: h (m) = bar × 10.197 ÷ SG
PSI to feet: h (ft) = psi × 2.31 ÷ SG
kPa to metres: h (m) = kPa × 0.10197 ÷ SG

Head is energy per unit weight of fluid, so a pump curve in metres stays valid for water, diesel or brine as long as viscosity is low.

The key input that changes from job to job is density, and in the field we usually work with specific gravity of the liquid instead. Water has SG 1.0, diesel is near 0.85 and a strong brine can exceed 1.2.

How to Convert Bar to Metres of Head?

To convert bar to metres of head, multiply the bar value by 10.197 and divide by the specific gravity of the liquid. For water at SG 1.0, 3 bar becomes about 30.6 m of head.

Solved Example: 5 Bar to Metres of Head

A gauge pressure of 5 bar on water gives about 50.99 m or 167.3 ft of head. The same 5 bar on diesel with SG 0.85 gives a larger head of about 59.98 m.

Given: P = 5 bar = 500000 Pa, SG = 1.0
ρ = 1.0 × 1000 = 1000 kg/m³
h = 500000 ÷ (1000 × 9.80665)
h = 50.99 m of water
In feet: 50.99 × 3.281 = 167.3 ft
Diesel, SG 0.85: 50.99 ÷ 0.85 = 59.98 m

Now try a psi example. A discharge reading of 100 psi on water equals 100 × 2.31 = 231 ft of head, while the same reading on a liquid with SG 1.2 equals only 192.5 ft.

How Does Specific Gravity Affect Head?

Specific gravity sits in the denominator, so a heavier liquid gives less head for the same pressure and a lighter liquid gives more.

Light hydrocarbons

Petrol near SG 0.72 gives about 14.2 m of head per bar.

Best for: Refinery pumps and tank farms
SG below 1
Water services

Cooling and fire water near SG 1.0 give about 10.2 m per bar.

Best for: Utilities and water treatment
SG near 1
Brines and acids

Strong sulphuric acid near SG 1.8 gives only about 5.7 m per bar.

Best for: Chemical dosing and process lines
SG above 1
Myth: A pump that makes 50 m on water will make 50 m worth of pressure on every liquid.
Fact: The head stays about the same for thin liquids, but the gauge pressure changes in direct proportion to specific gravity.
Myth: Head and pressure are two different forms of energy.
Fact: They are the same energy expressed per unit weight and per unit volume, linked only by density and gravity.
Myth: You can skip specific gravity if the liquid looks like water.
Fact: Glycol mixtures and brines look like water but can have SG of 1.05 to 1.2, which shifts head by up to 20 %.

Pump Head vs Pressure Gauge Reading

A pump is rated in head because head does not depend on the liquid, while a pressure gauge reads force per area, which does depend on the liquid. To compare them you must convert the gauge reading with the actual specific gravity.

MTH Pumps shows that one pump giving 33 ft of head produces about 14.7 psi on water but about 26.5 psi on sulphuric acid with SG 1.8.

Why engineers use head
  • One pump curve works for any thin liquid
  • Static lift and friction add directly in metres
  • Matches system curves
Why gauges show pressure
  • Pressure is what the sensor feels
  • Pipe and flange ratings are in bar
  • Relief valves are set in pressure units

In practice, total pump head is discharge head minus suction head, both converted from gauge readings and corrected for gauge elevation. To see how the impeller creates head, read about the centrifugal pump working principle, and our comparison of centrifugal vs positive displacement pumps explains why only centrifugal pumps are rated in head.

Suction conditions need absolute pressure instead of gauge pressure, so check the guide on NPSH and cavitation before you convert suction readings. The difference between the two references is explained in absolute vs gauge pressure.

How Many Feet of Head Is 1 PSI?

One psi of water equals about 2.31 ft of head, so you multiply psi by 2.31 and divide by specific gravity. The reverse factor is 0.433 psi per foot of water.

Where Is Pressure to Head Conversion Used in Plants?

Pressure to head conversion is used wherever a pressure reading must be compared with a height, a pump curve or a liquid level.

Pump performance checks
Convert suction and discharge gauge readings into total head and compare with the curve.
Hydrostatic level
A bottom mounted transmitter reads pressure that is converted to liquid height in the tank.
Overhead tank supply
Find the static head available at a ground floor tap from a roof tank.
Pipeline friction
Express friction loss in metres so it can be added to static lift.

In hydrostatic level measurement the transmitter does this conversion inside its scaling, and the range is set using the method in calculating DP level transmitter range. For pipeline work, friction loss from pipe pressure drop calculation is often converted to metres before it is added to the static lift.

Common Errors and Field Tips

Most wrong answers come from using the wrong pressure reference, the wrong density or a gauge that is mounted at a different height from the pump.

  • Confirm whether the reading is gauge or absolute pressure.
  • Use specific gravity at actual operating temperature, not at 15 °C.
  • Correct for gauge height above or below the pump centreline.
  • Do not mix kg/cm² and bar without the 0.981 factor.

Pressure to Head Conversion FAQ

What is pressure to head conversion in simple words with example?

It means expressing a pressure as the height of liquid that would create it, using h = P ÷ (ρg). For example, 2 bar on water equals about 20.4 m of head.

A lighter liquid needs a taller column for the same pressure, so 2 bar on diesel gives about 24 m.

What is the difference between pump head and pump discharge pressure?

Pump head is energy per unit weight in metres or feet, while discharge pressure is force per area in bar or psi. A pump giving 30 m of head shows about 2.94 bar on water.

The same pump on a liquid with SG 1.5 shows about 4.4 bar, yet the head stays near 30 m.

How to calculate head in metres from bar step by step?

Multiply bar by 100000 to get pascal, then divide by specific gravity times 9806.65. For 4 bar of water the answer is about 40.8 m of head.

A quick shortcut is bar × 10.2 ÷ SG, which is accurate enough for most field checks and pump tests.

Why does specific gravity change the head for the same pressure?

Pressure equals density times gravity times height, so a denser liquid reaches the same pressure with a shorter column. Doubling specific gravity halves the head.

This is why acid pumps show high gauge pressure even when their head rating is modest, and why SG must be written on every datasheet.

What are the common mistakes when converting pressure into liquid head?

The biggest mistakes are using absolute pressure instead of gauge, taking SG as 1.0 for every liquid and ignoring gauge elevation. Each can shift results by several metres.

Atmospheric pressure alone equals about 10.3 m of water head, so a wrong reference can make a pump look far stronger than it is.

Which unit is best for pump head in Indian process plants?

Metres of liquid column is the preferred unit, because most Indian pump datasheets follow SI units. Pressure gauges are usually in bar or kg/cm².

Keep one consistent unit on a project, and convert imperial vendor curves from feet to metres by dividing by 3.281.
