Turbine Meter vs Positive Displacement

Side by side comparison: how they work, where each fits, and which to choose.

Quick answer: Turbine meters suit clean, low viscosity fluids at high flows; positive displacement meters suit viscous liquids and low flows with high accuracy and no straight run.

Choose Turbine Meter when

  • Clean fuels, water and gases at high flow

Choose Positive Displacement when

  • Oils, syrups, resins and dosing
  • Low flows and fuel dispensing

Key Differences Explained

Both are mechanical meters with moving parts and pulse outputs. Turbines lose accuracy as viscosity rises; PD meters slip less as viscosity rises.

Both are widely used for custody transfer, proved with meter factors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Turbine Meter and Positive Displacement?

Turbine meters suit clean, low viscosity fluids at high flows; positive displacement meters suit viscous liquids and low flows with high accuracy and no straight run.

Why do turbine meters need a strainer?

Particles damage bearings and blades.

Is an oval gear meter a PD meter?

Yes, as are piston, nutating disc and rotary vane meters.

How is accuracy maintained?

By proving and applying a meter factor.
