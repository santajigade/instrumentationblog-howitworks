About this Table

NPS to DN chart for steel pipe, with the actual outside diameter in mm and inches for each size.

Basis: ISO 6708 DN designations and ASME B36.10M / B36.19M outside diameters.

How to use

  1. Find NPS in inches and read the DN.
  2. Use the OD for clamp on meters, flange and fitting selection.
  3. DN is a label; it is not the bore.

Notes

  • From NPS 14 the OD in inches equals the NPS.
  • EN 10255 and some metric tube systems use different ODs for the same DN.

Common Mistakes with NPS and DN

NPS and DN are size names, not measured dimensions. Up to NPS 12 the outside diameter is larger than the nominal size, so a 2 inch pipe is 60.3 mm OD, not 50.8 mm. The bore then depends on the wall thickness: the same NPS 2 pipe has a bore of about 52.5 mm in Schedule 40 and 49.2 mm in Schedule 80. Use the schedule and wall thickness, not the nominal size, when you enter the inside diameter for a flowmeter, orifice or velocity calculation.

Watch for systems that share a DN but not an outside diameter. Copper tube, hygienic stainless tube to DIN 11850 and metric hydraulic tube are sized by their actual OD, so a clamp on meter or fitting set up for 33.4 mm DN 25 steel pipe will not suit a 29 mm hygienic line. When ordering instruments, state the OD and wall thickness as well as the DN, especially for clamp on ultrasonic meters, inline magnetic meters and insertion probes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What DN is 2 inch pipe?

DN 50, with an OD of 60.3 mm.

What is DN 100 in inches?

NPS 4, OD 114.3 mm.

Is DN the inside diameter?

No. It is a nominal size; the bore depends on wall thickness.
